Islamic will (wasiyyah)

In the Sunnah, a Muslim who has something to bequeath should not spend two nights without a written will (Bukhārī; Muslim). The wasiyyah is the document that records debts, trusts, funeral wishes, and optional bequests.

What the will may contain

  • Payment of debts and trusts first.
  • A bequest of up to one-third of the remaining estate to non-heirs (or charity), unless heirs later consent.
  • Appointment of an executor (waṣī) and guardians for minors.
  • Funeral and burial wishes that do not contradict Sharīʿah.

What the will cannot do

  • It cannot re-cut the Qur’anic faraʾid shares among legal heirs.
  • It cannot cancel a debt Allah made binding.
