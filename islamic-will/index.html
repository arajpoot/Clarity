Islamic will (wasiyyah)

Legacy path · Day 5 of 7 · ← Day 4 · Day 6 →

One sentence: write debts, trusts, funeral wishes, and optional gifts — then let farāʾiḍ do what Allah already assigned.

Disclaimer. Not a court will. Not a fatwa. Civil probate differs. Use a solicitor/notary and a scholar.

Last reviewed: September 2026 · Bukhārī/Muslim encouragement to write a will — confirm text on Sunnah.com · shares in 4:11–12

Do this today

  • List debts, loans, and trusts you hold
  • Note optional bequest ideas for a scholar to review
  • Name a provisional executor to discuss with family and counsel

Order of payout (plain language)

  1. Reasonable funeral costs
  2. Debts and trusts
  3. Valid wasiyyah — classically discussed as up to one-third of what remains, usually to non-heirs
  4. Fixed heirs (farāʾiḍ)

The ⅓ rule in one paragraph

Many Sunni teachers explain that optional bequests should not exceed one-third of the net estate, and should not be used to enlarge a fixed heir’s share. Heirs consenting after death is a separate discussion. Do not draft that yourself from a webpage.

May / may not

May: funeral wishes that do not contradict Sharīʿah; help name an executor; optional portion within limits to charity or non-heirs.

May not: cancel Qur’anic shares by preference; ignore debts; replace your country’s will formalities.

Executor checklist

  • Someone trustworthy who can work with a lawyer
  • Knows where documents and keys are
  • Willing to pay debts before gifts

Country collision notes

Pakistan · United Kingdom · Canada — how civil probate meets this outline, not a second fiqh chapter.

FAQ

Is this page a valid will?

No. Prepare questions for a scholar and a lawyer.

Do I need both wasiyyah notes and a civil will?

Often yes outside Muslim-majority court systems.

Can I leave everything to one child?

That fights the farāʾiḍ map in 4:11–12. Ask a scholar; do not copy a custom.
