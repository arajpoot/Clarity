Islamic will (wasiyyah)

Legacy path · Day 5 of 7 · ← Day 4 · Day 6 →

One sentence: write debts, trusts, funeral wishes, and optional gifts — then let farāʾiḍ do what Allah already assigned.

Disclaimer. Not a court will. Not a fatwa. Civil probate differs. Use a solicitor/notary and a scholar.

Last reviewed: September 2026 · shares in Qur’an 4:11–12

Order of payout

  1. Reasonable funeral costs
  2. Debts and trusts
  3. Valid wasiyyah — classically discussed as up to one-third of what remains, usually to non-heirs
  4. Fixed heirs (farāʾiḍ)

May / may not

May: funeral wishes that do not contradict Sharīʿah; help name an executor; optional portion within limits to charity or non-heirs.

May not: cancel Qur’anic shares by preference; ignore debts; replace your country’s will formalities.

Country notes: Pakistan · UK · Canada. Alias: /wasiyyah/.

FAQ

Is this page a valid will?

No. Prepare questions for a scholar and a lawyer.

Do I need both wasiyyah notes and a civil will?

Often yes outside Muslim-majority court systems.
