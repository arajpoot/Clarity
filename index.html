Tajweed Learning Modules (Hafs ʿan ʿĀṣim)

Structured self-paced modules for the most widely used narration — Ḥafṣ ʿan ʿĀṣim. Complete one level before moving to the next. Free resources are linked inside each module. Pair with a teacher when possible for live correction and eventual ijāzah.

▶️ Learn Tajweed – the Easy Way (English)

Start with Lesson 1a – Introduction by Understand Al-Quran Academy (Dr. Abdulazeez Abdulraheem). Clear, short lessons for adults.

Full English playlist: Learn Tajweed – the Easy Way (English) · First lesson: Lesson 1a | Introduction

🌱 Module 1 – Beginner Foundations

Goal: Correctly pronounce all Arabic letters from their articulation points and apply the most basic Tajweed rules while reading short surahs.

Learning Objectives

  • Recognise and pronounce all 28 Arabic letters with correct makhārij
  • Master short vowels (fatḥah, kasrah, ḍammah) and long vowels (madd aṣlī)
  • Understand sukūn, shaddah, and tanwīn
  • Apply the simplest rules of Qalqalah and basic Ghunnah
  • Read Al-Fātiḥah and the short surahs (Al-Ikhlāṣ, Al-Falaq, An-Nās) with basic accuracy

Recommended Sequence (4–6 weeks)

  1. Week 1–2: Arabic alphabet + makhārij (throat, tongue, lips, nasal cavity)
  2. Week 2–3: Short & long vowels, tanwīn, sukūn, shaddah
  3. Week 3–4: Introduction to Qalqalah (echo letters) and basic Ghunnah
  4. Week 4–6: Practice reading Al-Fātiḥah and Juzʾ ʿAmma short surahs while applying the above

Free Resources for this Module

  • E-Quran Coaching – Free Tajweed Guide (Level 1 Fundamentals)
  • Tabarak Academy – Free Tajweed Course
  • Sufaraa Center – Free Lessons 1–33
  • Al Qasas – Free Alphabet & Makharij Packs
  • Learn Quran Tajwid App
  • YouTube: “Noorani Qaida with Tajweed”, “Learn Tajweed the Easy Way”

Do not rush. Correct makhārij are the foundation. Record yourself and compare with Al-Husary.

🌿 Module 2 – Intermediate Application

Goal: Fluently apply the core Tajweed rules (Noon & Meem Sākinah, all common Madd types, heavy/light letters, Qalqalah) while reading longer passages.

Learning Objectives

  • Master the four rules of Noon Sākinah & Tanwīn (Iẓhār, Idghām, Iqlāb, Ikhfāʾ)
  • Master the rules of Meem Sākinah
  • Apply all major types of Madd
  • Distinguish heavy (tafkhīm) and light (tarqīq) letters
  • Read fluently from Al-Mulk, Yā-Sīn, Ar-Raḥmān, Al-Kahf while applying rules

Recommended Sequence (6–10 weeks)

  1. Weeks 1–3: Noon Sākinah & Tanwīn rules
  2. Weeks 3–5: Meem Sākinah + Ghunnah
  3. Weeks 5–7: All types of Madd
  4. Weeks 7–9: Heavy & light letters + Qalqalah
  5. Weeks 9–10+: Continuous reading of medium-length surahs

Free Resources

  • E-Quran Coaching Guide – Intermediate
  • Sheikh Ayman Swayd full series
  • Quran.com – Colour-coded Tajweed + audio
  • TajweedMate App – AI feedback

A teacher’s ear becomes very valuable at this stage.

🌳 Module 3 – Advanced Refinement & Path to Ijāzah

Goal: Fluent, beautiful, rule-perfect recitation (tartīl) with correct waqf/ibtidāʾ and readiness for formal authorisation.

Learning Objectives

  • Master Waqf and Ibtidāʾ
  • Apply advanced Madd and Hafs-specific features
  • Recite with proper rhythm and beauty
  • Prepare for reading the entire Quran to a qualified teacher for ijāzah

Resources

  • Fountain Tajweed Institute – Hafs ʿan ʿĀṣim
  • Sheikh Ayman Swayd advanced lessons
  • Seek a teacher who holds an authentic ijāzah in Hafs ʿan ʿĀṣim

True excellence is transmitted orally. Free materials give knowledge; a living teacher gives the chain and precision.

General Tip: Consistency beats intensity. Even 15–25 focused minutes daily is better than long irregular sessions.

What Benefits the Deceased in the Grave — From the Scholars

Classical scholars emphasised that the living can benefit the dead through specific deeds that reach them by Allah’s permission. Below is a concise summary drawn from mainstream Sunni fiqh and the writings of major scholars.

Ibn Taymiyyah (d. 728 AH) — In Majmūʿ al-Fatāwā and related works he affirmed that the dead benefit from:
  • Duʿāʾ and istighfār made for them
  • Ṣadaqah given on their behalf
  • Hajj and ʿUmrah performed on their behalf (when conditions are met)
  • The reward of righteous deeds that the deceased themselves had set in motion (ongoing charity, knowledge that is benefited from, righteous children who pray for them)
He stressed that these reach the deceased by the will of Allah and that the best gift is continuous duʿāʾ and ṣadaqah.
Ibn al-Qayyim (d. 751 AH) — In Kitāb al-Rūḥ he collected authentic reports showing that the soul in the barzakh is aware of visits, benefits from the recitation of Qurʾān and from the good deeds of the living when done with sincerity for Allah and intended for the deceased. He warned against innovations while affirming the established benefits of duʿāʾ, charity and seeking forgiveness.
Imam al-Nawawi (d. 676 AH) and the Shāfiʿī school — Encouraged seeking forgiveness for the dead, giving charity on their behalf, and making duʿāʾ. He noted that the Prophet ﷺ himself sought forgiveness for the believers who had passed away.
Ibn ʿArabī (d. 638 AH) — While his metaphysical writings are specialised, he also emphasised the living connection of the believer’s heart with the Prophet ﷺ and the importance of constant remembrance and salutations (ṣalawāt). Many later scholars who drew from his spiritual insights stressed that the light of ṣalawāt and sincere dhikr becomes companionship for the soul after death.
Practical summary (majority view of Ahl al-Sunnah):
  • Duʿāʾ & Istighfār — the most agreed-upon and powerful gift
  • Ṣadaqah (including ongoing charity / waqf) on behalf of the deceased
  • Qurʾān recitation with the intention that its reward reaches them (widely accepted)
  • Ḥajj / ʿUmrah and fasting on their behalf when appropriate
  • Righteous offspring who continue to pray and do good
  • Ṣalawāt upon the Prophet ﷺ — a light that accompanies the believer
Note: These teachings are educational. For specific rulings on a particular case, consult a qualified local scholar. The goal is to send light ahead while we are still able to act.
