🌿 Ṭibb Nabawī — Prophetic medicine (one remedy)

Educational only — not medical advice or a fatwa. Always consult a qualified physician for health decisions. Verify primary texts on Sunnah.com.

Black seed (الحبّة السوداء) — al-ḥabba al-sawdāʾ

فِي الْحَبَّةِ السَّوْدَاءِ شِفَاءٌ مِنْ كُلِّ دَاءٍ إِلَّا السَّامَ

The Prophet ﷺ said: “In the black seed there is healing for every disease except death (as-sām).”

Bukhārī 5688 · Muslim 2215 · Open on Sunnah.com

What this is (classical note)

Scholars understood this as a general blessing in the seed (Nigella sativa), not a claim that it replaces every treatment by itself. Ibn Ḥajar and others noted that “every disease” can mean many illnesses in the practice of the Arabs of that time, or a broad benefit with exceptions known to Allah — and that as-sām (death) is explicitly excluded.

Contemporary Islamic commentary (educational)
  • Modern Muslim physicians and herbal educators often discuss black seed oil/seed as a supportive wellness food — never as a substitute for diagnosis or prescribed care.
  • Prophetic medicine is studied as part of the Sunnah of prevention, moderation, and trust in Allah (tawakkul), alongside seeking treatment (the Prophet ﷺ encouraged medicine).
  • For deeper reading: authentic ḥadīth collections first; then works cataloguing aṭ-ṭibb an-nabawī (e.g. Ibn al-Qayyim’s discussions) under a teacher — not internet self-prescription.
Verify & learn more: Bukhārī 5688 · Muslim 2215 · Sunnah.com search · IslamQA (edu context)

Other well-attested Prophetic remedies in the rotation: honey, dates (ʿajwah), cupping (ḥijāmah), and the principle of seeking treatment.

⚰️ Ṣalāt al-Janāzah (funeral prayer)

Educational outline — not a fatwa. Schools differ on details (loud/silent Fātiḥah, hands, number of takbīrs in some reports).
  • Ḥanafī: typically four takbīrs; Fātiḥah not required as in daily prayer in the same way — follow your text/imam.
  • Mālikī / Shāfiʿī / Ḥanbalī: details of Fātiḥah and duʿāʾ placement vary — study your madhhab.
  • In congregation: do what the imam does and ask him afterward if unsure.
Stand if able; no rukūʿ or sujūd. Confirm with your imam or local scholar.
  1. Intention in the heart for the funeral prayer for this deceased, following the imam if in congregation.
  2. First takbīr — then recite Sūrat al-Fātiḥah (many also recite a short sūrah; schools differ).
  3. Second takbīr — send ṣalawāt upon the Prophet ﷺ (as in the final tashahhud of the daily prayer).
  4. Third takbīr — make duʿāʾ for the deceased (and often for all Muslims). Example: the long duʿāʾ beginning with Allāhumma ighfir li-ḥayyinā wa mayyitinā…
  5. Fourth takbīr — brief pause / short duʿāʾ, then salām to the right (and left, according to the school).
Core duʿāʾ after the third takbīr (widely taught)

اللَّهُمَّ اغْفِرْ لَهُ وَارْحَمْهُ وَعَافِهِ وَاعْفُ عَنْهُ وَأَكْرِمْ نُزُلَهُ وَوَسِّعْ مُدْخَلَهُ وَاغْسِلْهُ بِالْمَاءِ وَالثَّلْجِ وَالْبَرَدِ وَنَقِّهِ مِنَ الْخَطَايَا كَمَا يُنَقَّى الثَّوْبُ الْأَبْيَضُ مِنَ الدَّنَسِ وَأَبْدِلْهُ دَارًا خَيْرًا مِنْ دَارِهِ وَأَهْلًا خَيْرًا مِنْ أَهْلِهِ وَزَوْجًا خَيْرًا مِنْ زَوْجِهِ وَأَدْخِلْهُ الْجَنَّةَ وَأَعِذْهُ مِنْ عَذَابِ الْقَبْرِ وَمِنْ عَذَابِ النَّارِ

O Allah, forgive him/her, have mercy on him/her, give him/her peace and pardon him/her. Make his/her rest honourable and his/her entry spacious. Wash him/her with water, snow and hail, and cleanse him/her of sins as a white garment is cleansed of dirt. Exchange his/her home for a better home, family for a better family, and spouse for a better spouse. Admit him/her to Paradise and protect him/her from the punishment of the grave and of the Fire. (Muslim)

After the prayer: bury without delay when possible, make duʿāʾ at the grave, and continue ṣadaqah, Qurʾān, and istighfār on their behalf. Further reading (IslamQA overview) →

Preparing the Deceased & Islamic Burial — Process & Resources

When a Muslim dies, the community has a collective duty (farḍ kifāyah) to wash, shroud, pray over, and bury the body with dignity and without unnecessary delay. Below is a concise overview and trusted online resources for Muslim-majority and non-Muslim countries.

Core steps (Sunnah process)
  1. Confirm death and notify family / local mosque / Islamic funeral service promptly.
  2. Ghusl — ritual washing by same-gender Muslims (or trained helpers).
  3. Kafan — shrouding in simple white cloth (typically 3 layers for men, 5 for women).
  4. Ṣalāt al-Janāzah — funeral prayer (standing, no rukūʿ/sujūd).
  5. Burial (dafn) — preferably in a Muslim cemetery or Muslim section; body on the right side facing the Qiblah; haste is recommended.

Avoid cremation. Embalming is generally avoided when possible; scholars allow exceptions for legal/transport necessity.

In Muslim countries
  • Local mosques and municipal Islamic burial offices usually handle ghusl, kafan, janāzah and cemetery allocation quickly.
  • Follow local health and civil registration requirements (death certificate) while keeping the ritual sequence intact.
  • Contact your neighbourhood mosque or the national Islamic affairs authority for the nearest facility.
In non-Muslim countries
  • Contact the nearest mosque or a Muslim funeral director experienced in Islamic rites as soon as possible.
  • Prefer burial where the person died (local Muslim cemetery or Muslim section). Repatriation is costly, often requires embalming or sealed caskets, and delays burial — many scholars discourage it unless there is a clear need.
  • If only a non-Muslim cemetery is available, bury there under necessity (ḍarūrah); request Qiblah orientation and a plot near other Muslims when possible.
  • Obtain the legal burial permit / death certificate; funeral directors familiar with Islamic practice coordinate paperwork, ghusl facilities, and cemetery booking.
Online guides — burial process
  • Preparation of the Deceased (PDF) – IslamHouse
  • Alim.org – Islamic funeral procedure (ghusl to burial)
  • Gardens of Peace (UK) – What to do when someone dies (PDF)
  • IslamOnline – Burying a Muslim in a non-Muslim cemetery
  • IMAM-US – Islamic laws of death and burial
Waṣiyyah (Islamic will) — fulfilling commands before and after death

The Prophet ﷺ encouraged writing a will. A Muslim may bequeath up to one-third of the estate; the rest follows fixed Qurʾānic inheritance shares (farāʾiḍ) after debts and funeral costs. The will should also record funeral wishes, unpaid religious obligations (missed prayers/fasts, zakat, Hajj), trusts held for others, and an executor (waṣī).

  • ANIC (Australia) – Last Islamic Will (Wasiyah) template
  • FreeIslamicWill.com – Sample will templates
  • The Blessed Nights – Islamic will generator
  • Try Barakah – How to write an Islamic will (guide)
  • Shariawiz – Sharīʿah-compliant will tools
  • IslamQA – Ruling on writing a will

Have any will reviewed by a local scholar and a lawyer so it is both Sharīʿah-compliant and legally enforceable in your country. Store copies with family, the executor, and your lawyer.

Note: This summary is educational. Procedures and civil law differ by country. Always consult a qualified local scholar and an experienced Muslim funeral service for your situation.

About Clarity

Clarity is a local-first companion to help you prepare the heart and the grave — with Quran, authentic Hadith, character lessons, and small daily deeds. It is not a full Quran library, prayer engine, or fatwa service.

✦ Positioning

Clarity sits beside the great libraries (Quran.com, Sunnah.com, IslamQA) — not instead of them. Its focus is turning knowledge into consistent, grave-conscious practice and practical legacy tools (family, will, Hajj readiness) while keeping your data private on this device.

Educational only. For personal rulings, consult a qualified scholar.

  • Quran — live via Quran.com / AlQuran.cloud APIs (Uthmani + Sahih International).

  • Hadith — random/search via UmmahAPI; always prefer linking to Sunnah.com for verification.

Imām Abū Ḥāmid al-Ghazālī (d. 505 AH) — From Iḥyāʾ ʿUlūm al-Dīn (Book of the Remembrance of Death and the Afterlife):
  • He taught that constant remembrance of death softens the heart, reduces attachment to the world, and prepares the soul for the grave. “Remember often the destroyer of pleasures (death).”
  • The grave itself addresses the deceased: if the person was obedient, it becomes a garden of mercy and light; if rebellious, a place of constriction. Good deeds done in life answer on behalf of the believer.
  • The living benefit the dead through sincere duʿāʾ, seeking forgiveness, charity given on their behalf, and the reward of Qurʾān and righteous actions intended for them.
  • He urged visiting graves to awaken the heart and to send light ahead while one is still able to act.

Ibn Taymiyyah (d. 728 AH) — In Majmūʿ al-Fatāwā and related works he affirmed that the dead benefit from:
  • Duʿāʾ and istighfār made for them
  • Ṣadaqah given on their behalf
  • Hajj and ʿUmrah performed on their behalf (when conditions are met)
  • The reward of righteous deeds that the deceased themselves had set in motion (ongoing charity, knowledge that is benefited from, righteous children who pray for them)
He stressed that these reach the deceased by the will of Allah and that the best gift is continuous duʿāʾ and ṣadaqah.
Ibn al-Qayyim (d. 751 AH) — In Kitāb al-Rūḥ he collected authentic reports showing that the soul in the barzakh is aware of visits, benefits from the recitation of Qurʾān and from the good deeds of the living when done with sincerity for Allah and intended for the deceased. He warned against innovations while affirming the established benefits of duʿāʾ, charity and seeking forgiveness.
Imam al-Nawawi (d. 676 AH) and the Shāfiʿī school — Encouraged seeking forgiveness for the dead, giving charity on their behalf, and making duʿāʾ. He noted that the Prophet ﷺ himself sought forgiveness for the believers who had passed away.
Ibn ʿArabī (d. 638 AH) — While his metaphysical writings are specialised, he also emphasised the living connection of the believer’s heart with the Prophet ﷺ and the importance of constant remembrance and salutations (ṣalawāt). Many later scholars who drew from his spiritual insights stressed that the light of ṣalawāt and sincere dhikr becomes companionship for the soul after death.
Practical summary (majority view of Ahl al-Sunnah):
  • Duʿāʾ & Istighfār — the most agreed-upon and powerful gift
  • Ṣadaqah (including ongoing charity / waqf) on behalf of the deceased
  • Qurʾān recitation with the intention that its reward reaches them (widely accepted)
  • Ḥajj / ʿUmrah and fasting on their behalf when appropriate
  • Righteous offspring who continue to pray and do good
  • Ṣalawāt upon the Prophet ﷺ — a light that accompanies the believer

🔥 Warnings of the Fire< deeds Allah linked to severe punishment

This is a reminder, not a fatwa. The Prophet ﷺ named seven destructive sins (al-mūbiqāt) in a report agreed upon by al-Bukhārī and Muslim. Other verses and ṣaḥīḥ reports also attach a warning of the Fire to specific crimes. A believer who dies upon unrepented shirk is not forgiven; other major sins are under Allah’s will — He may punish or pardon — and sincere tawbah is accepted before death. Verify personal cases with a trusted scholar.

Core ḥadīth: Abū Hurayrah reported that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: “Avoid the seven destructive sins.” They asked, “What are they, O Messenger of Allah?” He said: “Associating partners with Allah; sorcery; killing a soul Allah has forbidden except by right; consuming ribā; consuming an orphan’s wealth; fleeing the battlefield; and slandering chaste, believing women who are unaware.” — Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 2766 / 6857, Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 89.

How scholars describe tawbah (repentance)

Imām al-Nawawī (Riyāḍ al-Ṣāliḥīn) and the majority: tawbah is obligatory from every sin. If the sin is only between the servant and Allah, three conditions; if it violates a human right, a fourth is added.

1. Iqlaʿ
Stop the sin at once.
2. Nadam
Feel genuine remorse.
3. ʿAzm
Resolve never to return.
4. Ḥaqq al-ādamī
Return wealth, honour, or seek pardon if a person was wronged.

Window of acceptance: before the soul reaches the throat, and before the sun rises from the west (ḥadīth in Muslim and others). Allah says: “Say: O My servants who have transgressed against themselves, do not despair of the mercy of Allah. Indeed Allah forgives all sins. He is the Forgiving, the Merciful.” (al-Zumar 39:53). And: “…except those who repent, believe, and do righteous deeds — for them Allah will replace their evil deeds with good deeds.” (al-Furqān 25:70).

Also: Anas reported the qudsī ḥadīth: “O son of Ādam, were you to come to Me with an earthful of sins and then meet Me without having associated anything with Me, I would come to you with an earthful of pardon.” (al-Tirmidhī 3540; graded ḥasan by many, including al-Albānī). Ibn al-Qayyim: tawbah is not a phrase on the tongue only — it is leaving, regretting, and replacing harm with obedience.

📜 Wasiyyah — Islamic will & bequest

💡 How to draft a Wasiyyah · quick guide
  1. Order of rights — burial/funeral costs and debts first; then optional bequests; then farāʾiḍ for heirs.
  2. ⅓ rule — optional wasiyyah to non-heirs / charity is capped at one-third of the net (unless heirs consent).
  3. Do not use bequests to increase a fixed-share heir’s portion beyond the Qurʾān.
  4. Guardians & executor (waṣī) — pick trusted adults from the tree or type names; name an alternate.
  5. Save / print — device-local draft only; formal wills need local legal + scholarly review.

Educational draft only — not a fatwa, not a notarized will. Align with Qurʾān 4:11–12 and local law; ask a scholar and, where required, a lawyer. Farāʾiḍ calculator above is a Hanafi study aid — other madhāhib differ; no binding distribution.
