What benefits the deceased in Islam

Legacy path · Day 2 of 7 · ← Day 1 · Day 4 →

One sentence: some of what they planted continues; some of what you gift may reach them; duʿāʾ is asked of the living.

What the sources say

Abū Hurayrah reported that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that when a person dies, his deeds end except three: ongoing charity, beneficial knowledge, and a righteous child who prays for him. Read: sunnah.com/muslim:1631.

Three pipes

  1. What they planted — ṣadaqah jāriyah, knowledge they taught, a child they raised to pray.
  2. What you gift — charity given on their behalf, as many scholars teach.
  3. What you pray — duʿāʾ for forgiveness and mercy. See duʿāʾ pages.

How schools differ

Qur’an recited and gifted to the dead is discussed with nuance. Many later Sunni teachers allow it; some voices were more reserved. This page does not settle that. Ask your imam.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming a paid khatm replaces a life of ṣalāh
  • Neglecting duʿāʾ because “we hired someone”
  • Ignoring debts they left

FAQ

Is charity for the dead allowed?

Many scholars allow ṣadaqah on behalf of the deceased. Confirm with your teacher.

Does Muslim 1631 list only three things?

It lists three that continue from what the person left. Other reports discuss duʿāʾ and charity from others — study both with a scholar.

Can I recite Qur’an for them?

Schools differ. Follow your local imam; do not treat this page as a verdict.
